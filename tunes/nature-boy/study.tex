\begin{study}{Lesson notes (Session 2, Nick)}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item Latin jazz is a mix: samba, bossa, Cuban, all based on the bell pattern (clave).
  Samba and Cuban clave are two bars, one ``on'' and one ``off'' (or the other way around).
  Samba rhythm is like \emph{Oye como va}. Cuban clave: 1 2 3 4; bossa clave.
\item Bars 5--8: voice leading, descending line (\mel{1\;7\;\flat7\;6} under \ch{i}{-}),
  which Nick calls a ``Valentino'' (as in \emph{My Funny Valentine}).
\item Spanish Phrygian: fifth mode of the harmonic minor, resolving a dominant to the minor chord.
\item Harmonic minor: always when preparing the resolution on the root; also in bar 13
  (\ch{II}{7\flat9}: it does not resolve to the fifth, it goes on to the dominant of the root).
\item Rule of thumb: on a cadence, join the thirds or join the fifths.
\end{itemize}
\end{study}

\begin{study}{Chromatic moments}
Chords outside D natural minor in \textcolor{chromatic}{red}; the melody note sounding
over them is circled, with its function on the chord:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item bars 5--6 (and 21--22), the ``Valentino'' line \ch{i}{-} \chx{i}{-\Delta7} \ch{i}{-7} \chx{i}{-6}:
  the melody sits on the moving voice, \mel{\tg{7}} = major 7th of \chx{i}{-\Delta7}, \mel{\tg{6}} = major 6th of \chx{i}{-6};
\item bar 7: \chx{iv}{-6} (major 6th \mel{2}, from D melodic minor); the melody stays on \mel{4\;\flat6};
\item bars 13--14: \chx{II}{7\flat9} (V/V): melody \mel{\tg{\flat3}} = $\flat$9, then \mel{\tg{\flat\lo{7}}} = $\flat$13;
  then on to \ch{V}{7} in bar 15 (see lesson notes on the harmonic minor);
\item bars 29--30: \chx{II}{7\flat9} with \mel{\tg{\flat3}} = $\flat$9, then \chx{V}{7\sharp5} with \mel{\tg{\flat3}} = $\sharp$5,
  resolving to \mel{1}.
\end{itemize}
\end{study}
