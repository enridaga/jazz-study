\begin{study}{Study notes}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item American songbook: learn from one song and look for the same things in other songs.
\item Common forms: AABA, ABAC (Tangerine is ABAC).
\item Lyrics can help: check for the rhymes.
\item Use the harmonic minor when preparing for \ch{ii}{-} and \ch{vi}{-}.
\item The main key is a planet and the others are moons. In Tangerine:
  \ch{I}{} and \ch{IV}{} major, \ch{ii}{-} and \ch{vi}{-} minor. Find the way to know when to play what.
\end{itemize}
\ideasfrom{a lesson at Jazzschool (Session 1, Pete)}
\end{study}

\begin{study}{Chromatic moments}
Chords outside F major in \textcolor{chromatic}{red}; the melody note sounding over them
is circled, with its function on the chord.
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item The dominant \chx{VI}{7} (V/ii) before every A: the melody leans on its tensions,
  \mel{\tg{1}} = $\sharp$9 (bars 4, 20, 32), \mel{\tg{4}} = $\flat$13 (bars 8, 16), \mel{\tg{5}} = $\sharp$9 (bar 24).
\item Bars 12--16, modulation to A major (\chx{III}{}): \chx{\sharp iv}{\text{\o}7} \chx{VII}{7\sharp9} $\to$ \chx{III}{}
  is a ii--V--I in A, with \mel{\tg{1}} = $\flat$9 of \chx{VII}{7}. In A the melody sits on
  \mel{\tg{\sharp1}} = the 3rd of A (C$\sharp$ in F terms is the new note); bar 14 \mel{\tg{2}} = $\sharp$9 of \chx{VII}{7};
  then \chx{III}{7} $\to$ \chx{VI}{7} brings it back to F.
\item Bar 23: \chx{\flat VII}{7} with \mel{\tg{3}} = $\sharp$11.
\item Bar 26: \chx{III}{7\sharp9} (V/vi) resolving to \ch{vi}{-}.
\item Bar 28: \chx{II}{7}, the major II (V/V): the exception to ``\ch{ii}{-} is minor''. Its 3rd is
  \mel{\sharp4}; the melody has \mel{\tg{\lo{7}}} = 13.
\end{itemize}
\end{study}
